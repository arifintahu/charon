### Counterfactual — what if we removed the LLM?

- Rejected candidates (WATCH/PASS, 1,381 over 5d) have **no outcome data**. The pipeline drops them at the LLM gate, no candidate is paper-traded post-rejection, so there's no way to ask "did we miss winners?" from the existing tables. We'd need to either (a) flip `use_llm: false` for a window and let the gate-passing filter cohort trade, or (b) backtest with `--unscreened-policy=approve` to estimate.
- Within the LLM-approved-and-traded pool (667 trades), randomly picking from the 60-69 / 70-79 / 80+ buckets in proportion to their sizes gives the same expected PnL as letting the LLM pick. The LLM's *ranking* power is zero — it's just gating, and the gate is leaky (lets through plenty of losers).

### Cost vs benefit

- Model: `nvidia/nemotron-3-super-120b-a12b:free` via OpenRouter (`LLM_BASE_URL=https://openrouter.ai/api/v1`). The `:free` suffix is OpenRouter's no-charge tier — direct API cost is **$0.00/day**.
- Other costs: ~566 calls/day adds rate-limit pressure on the free tier (OpenRouter free models throttle hard), and each call adds 1–3s of latency before the candidate enters the buy queue. For degen, where rugs happen in 20–45 seconds, that latency is non-trivial — by the time the LLM responds, the entry price may already be staler than it was at signal time.
- So the dollar cost is zero, but the **latency tax** is real and the **opportunity cost** of being throttled by the LLM tier is real.

### Investigation queries (for reproducibility)

```sql
-- Throughput
SELECT verdict, COUNT(*) AS n, ROUND(AVG(confidence)::NUMERIC, 1) AS avg_conf
FROM llm_decisions
WHERE created_at_ms >= (EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM NOW())::BIGINT - 604800) * 1000

       ROUND(AVG(p.pnl_percent)::NUMERIC, 2) AS avg_pnl,

JOIN llm_decisions d ON p.machine_id=d.machine_id AND p.llm_decision_local_id=d.local_id
WHERE p.status='closed' AND p.strategy_id='degen'
  AND p.closed_at_ms >= (EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM NOW())::BIGINT - 604800) * 1000
GROUP BY bin ORDER BY bin;

-- Correlation
SELECT CORR(d.confidence, p.pnl_percent) AS pearson_r
FROM dry_run_positions p
JOIN llm_decisions d ON p.machine_id=d.machine_id AND p.llm_decision_local_id=d.local_id
WHERE p.status='closed' AND p.strategy_id='degen'
  AND p.closed_at_ms >= (EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM NOW())::BIGINT - 604800) * 1000;
```

### SL=-10% width check (the load-bearing finding)

For all 542 SL trades over 7d, max gain reached before SL fired (computed from `high_water_price / entry_price - 1`):

| Stat | Value |
|---|---|
| Median max-gain before SL | **+10.64%** |
| P75 max-gain | +30.50% |
| P90 max-gain | +59.10% |
| Trades that touched ≥+10% then SL'd | 278 (51%) |
| Trades that touched ≥+20% then SL'd | 186 (34%) |
| Trades that touched ≥+30% then SL'd (TP threshold) | **139 (26%)** |
| Trades that touched ≥+40% before SL | 100 (18%) |

**Half of all SL exits first traveled +10% in our direction.** A quarter touched +30% (the TP threshold, which arms the trail) and then fell back through entry to -10%. That's 139 trades over 7d that hit the trail-arm price and still got stopped out — and once armed, the trail at 15% would have locked at least ~+15% on each.

The current `tp_percent: 30, sl_percent: -10, trailing_percent: 15` config creates a 40-point round-trip requirement: price must move +30% (arm trail), then can give back 15% (locks +15% gain), or fall back -10% from entry. Many tokens spike +20% to +28%, drift, and then fall through -10% — too narrow a corridor.

**Opinion (strong): SL=-10% is wrong, but not for the reason you think.** It's not "too tight to let recoveries happen" — the recoveries are killed by the trail-arm threshold being too high (price has to reach exactly +30% before any downside protection kicks in). Two fixes:

1. **Lower the trail-arm threshold:** arm the trail at +15% or +20%, not +30%. This locks in *some* gain on the 186 trades that touched +20% and then died. Concretely, add a `trailing_arm_percent` field to the schema separate from `tp_percent`, default 15.
2. **If keeping arm = TP:** widen SL to -15% AND lower TP to 20%. SL=-15% costs an extra 5pp on the 264 SLs that never recovered, but lets the +15%-then-drop trades survive. TP=20% arms the trail sooner so the trail catches profit on the +20% spike-and-fade cohort.

Either fix should add ~5-10pp to the win rate without changing entry filters. Strongly recommend backtesting both before shipping.
