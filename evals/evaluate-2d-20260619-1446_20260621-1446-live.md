# Deep-dive follow-up (2026-06-21, interactive session)

Investigation beyond the standard battery, triggered by "some live positions have big SL due to token dump." Conclusions feed [[project_degen_optimization_frontier]].

## 1. Realized-vs-mark slippage by exit reason

`pnl_percent` is realized SOL from the actual Jupiter sell; `exit_price`/`exit_mcap` are only the mark at the trigger tick. The gap between them is execution slippage:

| exit | n | avg mark move | avg realized | slippage gap | gap >15pp |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TRAILING_TP | 41 | +34.1% | +31.5% | −2.6pp | 0 |
| SL | 28 | −1.6% | −17.2% | **−15.6pp** | **9** |

Winners fill clean (sell into buyers). Losers fill 15.6pp worse than the mark (worst −81pp) — the stop does **not** hold at −10%; dumping tokens fill into a thin/empty book.

## 2. The −97% case (HIV3) — rug, not a deep stop

Entry mcap 81k, **peaked at +22.3%** (high-water mcap 99k), then the LP was pulled at 38 min. Mark fell only −19.8% but the real sell returned ~2.5% of principal (−97.5%). Two structural facts:
- **The +22% was a phantom.** Live trailing arms off Jupiter's liquidity-adjusted *native* PnL (`pnlOverride`, positions.js:~145), which never crossed `trailing_arm_percent`=15 — so trailing correctly never armed. The thin pool couldn't realize the mcap-implied gain. `partial_tp` is off, so nothing was banked.
- A rug is an atomic LP removal; price gaps past every stop level in one block. No stop value beats it. This is the priced-in rug tail ([[project_degen_rug_tail]]).

## 3. LEVELZERO repeated SLs — whipsaw on a climber, NOT a dump

7 consecutive SLs (−4% to −14%) looked like a dumping token; it was the opposite — mcap **climbed 196k→271k (+38%)** while whipsawing the −10% stop on 0.4–8 min holds. The bot re-buys because degen entries come from the trending poller with only a 5-min *time-bucket* dedup (orchestrator.js:276), outcome-blind; the only position guard blocks re-entry while a position is open. Filter analysis:

| cohort | n | win rate | net SOL |
|---|---|---|---|
| First-ever entry on a mint | 22 | 50% | +0.072 |
| Later non-re-entry | 25 | 56% | +0.042 |
| Re-entry <60min after same-mint SL | 22 | **23%** | **+0.048** |

Re-entry-after-SL halves the win rate but is **net-positive** — the window's biggest winners all came after 2–4 consecutive same-mint SLs (TREMP **+99.7% after 4 SLs**, SIPPO +91% after 3, PEVOLUTION +86% after 2). LEVELZERO is the unlucky tail of a +EV pattern. **Do not add a per-mint re-entry cooldown** — it deletes more winner-SOL than it saves ([[project_degen_reentry_after_sl]]).

## 4. Rug tail is unfilterable — re-confirmed on live

- **Liquidity interleaving:** sorted by Jupiter (tradeable) liquidity, rugs sit among the biggest winners. GTAUSA **+256.7%** (best trade) had **thinner** liquidity (9.6k) than the HIV3 rug (10.7k); CraftWorld rug (13.2k) sat above four top runners. No liquidity floor removes the rugs without removing the bigger winners first.
- **Full feature set tested:** LP `gmgn.locked_ratio` is **uniformly 0.000** (dead), holder count is *inverted* (rugs higher), top-holder concentration overlaps 54–60% everywhere, `trending.bundler_rate`/`rug_ratio` are null 100%, token age overlaps. Nothing separates — consistent with the 14d priors in llm.js (insider/sniper % inverted). A real rug filter needs a *new* signal the bot doesn't capture (pool-reserve monitoring, deployer-wallet behavior); poor ROI vs a small priced-in tail. Only `position_size_sol` impacts rug *cost*, not rate.

## 5. Liquidity gate calibration (GMGN vs Jupiter)

The entry liquidity gate reads **GMGN** liquidity (candidateBuilder.js:231), which is a **constant ~1.98×** Jupiter's number across all 25 positions — GMGN = two-sided pool TVL, Jupiter = one-sided/quote-side depth (what your sell draws down). Net: `min_liquidity_usd: 16000` is an **~8k floor** in tradeable terms; `min_mcap_to_liquidity_ratio: 2.2` is effectively ~4.4 vs one-sided liquidity. This is a calibration/units honesty issue, **not** a rug-prevention lever (winners are equally thin). Flag, don't rush.

## 6. Where PnL leverage actually remains

Entry side and loser side are exhausted — keep as-is. The only unmilked leverage is **winner-exit capture**: winners peak at **+73.6%** but exit at **+46.9%** — capturing only **52% of the move** (26.7pp give-back per winner). `trailing_arm_percent` (15) and `partial_tp` (off) are unswept on live. **Do not tune now** — 30 winners / 2 days with 2–3 outliers driving the sum is noise-fitting. Freeze config, accumulate a clean live sample (~60+ winners), then run one focused exit-capture study. Until then the highest-value action is collecting data, not changing parameters.
